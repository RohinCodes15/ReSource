# Educator feedback plan

## Purpose

Learn whether the proposed resource-sharing workflow addresses a real need, what would make it trustworthy and usable, and what should change before implementation with real organizations. This is a plan only: no interviews, pilot, participation, endorsement, or validation are claimed.

## Ethical and practical guardrails

- Ask an adult mentor/teacher to help identify appropriate educators and school/community contacts.
- Explain that ReSource is a student project concept, not a district tool or FUSD partnership.
- Ask permission before taking notes or recording. Do not collect student names, student records, addresses, account credentials, or confidential purchasing information.
- Participation is voluntary; let people skip questions or stop. Keep notes de-identified and report themes, not attributed quotes, unless explicit permission is given.
- Do not enter real requests or inventory into the prototype. Use only its fictional sample data.
- Share what was learned and how it changed the product; do not turn interest in a demo into a claim of endorsement.

## Short questionnaire

1. Which role or perspective are you answering from? (Optional: teacher, operations, school leader, nonprofit/community partner, other, prefer not to say.)
2. How do classrooms or organizations currently handle unused supplies and unmet material needs?
3. Which part of the ReSource workflow, if any, would be useful? Why?
4. What would make a recommended match relevant—or clearly unsuitable?
5. Which condition, model/specification, quantity, timing, or location details must be visible before deciding?
6. What would you need to know before sharing inventory or approving a transfer?
7. Who should be allowed to submit requests, see inventory, approve a transfer, confirm receipt, and verify an avoided purchase?
8. What privacy, safety, accessibility, transportation, storage, or workload concerns should the design address?
9. Are the estimated-value and verified-value labels understandable? What wording would be clearer?
10. What one change would make this idea more useful or trustworthy?

Optional rating (1–5 plus “not sure”): ease of understanding; relevance of recommendations; confidence in the handoff; clarity of the impact figures.

## Lightweight process

With mentor support, invite a small, varied set of adults; give a brief explanation and the fictional guided demo; let participants explore without leading them to a positive answer; ask the questionnaire; summarize recurring themes and disagreements; record the date, roles (not names), method, sample limitations, and resulting product changes. Seek broader review before claiming representativeness. Any formal research, school access, or collection of sensitive information needs appropriate adult and institutional guidance first.
